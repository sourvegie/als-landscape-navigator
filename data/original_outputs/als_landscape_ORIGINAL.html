Key observations

Generated by fixed rules from the data below (no language model). Check each one against the linked trials before using it.

  • Most active mechanisms (active drug trials): Neuroinflammation & immune (6), SOD1 (5), Excitotoxicity (4).
  • Genetically defined or target-specific programmes (SOD1, C9orf72, FUS, ATXN2, TDP-43 biology) account for 9 of 106 active drug trials (8%); most activity still targets broad downstream pathways.
  • Advanced modalities (oligonucleotides, gene therapy, antibodies, cells) appear in 33 active trials; small molecules in 60.
  • TDP-43 pathology is present in the large majority of ALS cases; 3 drug trials all-time target this biology, 2 of them active (see deep-dive).
  • Strong genetic evidence but no active trial mapped in this snapshot: UBQLN2, TBK1, SQSTM1, ANG, TUBA4A, DCTN1, VCP, VAPB - candidate white space (or programmes still preclinical).
  • Phase 2+ primary-completion dates in the window: 2026: 1, 2027: 30, 2028: 12. These are potential data readouts to track.
  • 24 active drug trials (23%) have at least one site in Asia.

6 · Deep-dive: TDP-43 biology

TDP-43 mislocalisation and aggregation appear in the large majority of ALS cases, both sporadic and familial. That makes it the broadest disease-biology target in ALS, but it is hard to drug. Programmes here include direct approaches (antibodies, including AAV-vectorised antibodies) and approaches that restore TDP-43 function downstream (e.g. STMN2). 7 · Method & validation

Each drug intervention is classified by an ordered list of rules: 28 rules for named assets, then 18 keyword rules, then a fallback to the registry's intervention type. The rule that fired is saved in data/trials.csv, so every label can be traced. Placebo/sham arms and non-drug interventions are excluded from the landscape.
