# Programme assessments (provisional, pending scientific review)

**Status: PROVISIONAL – pending scientific review.** These notes were drafted by an AI assistant on 2026-11-20 from the cached ClinicalTrials.gov snapshot (retrieved 2026-11-18) and from public sources found the same day. No human scientist has reviewed them. They describe evidence. They contain no investment conclusions and should not be read as recommendations.

**Evidence labels used below**
- *Registry*: the ClinicalTrials.gov record as it stood in the snapshot.
- *Peer-reviewed*: a journal publication.
- *Company-reported*: a sponsor press release or conference presentation, usually reached through trade-press coverage. These results have not been independently verified here.
- *Trade press*: a news summary. It can lose detail.

The three programmes cover three different mechanism types: a downstream TDP-43 approach (QRL-201), a protein-level SOD1 approach that extends into sporadic ALS (AP-101), and a non-genetic bioenergetic approach with a regulatory filing already made (CNM-Au8).

## 1. QRL-201 (QurAlis): ASO intended to restore STMN2

| | |
|---|---|
| Registry record | [NCT05633459](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05633459) (ANQUR). Status in snapshot: active, not recruiting. Phase 1. n = 69 (actual). Primary completion 2026-05-29 (*estimated*; this date had passed by the analysis date). No results posted. |
| Navigator labels | TDP-43 biology / ASO. Curated direct target **STMN2**. This is not a direct TARDBP relationship. |

**Biological rationale.** When nuclear TDP-43 function is lost, a cryptic polyadenylation and splice site in the first intron of *STMN2* pre-mRNA is exposed. The result is a truncated, non-functional transcript and lower stathmin-2, a protein involved in axon growth and repair. Reduced stathmin-2 has been found in motor neurons from sporadic and C9orf72 ALS (*peer-reviewed*: [Melamed et al., Nat Neurosci 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30643298/); [Klim et al., Nat Neurosci 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-018-0300-4)). QRL-201 is an antisense oligonucleotide intended to correct this mis-processing and restore STMN2 expression (*company-reported*: [QurAlis, Feb 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260224163772/en)).

**Trial design and endpoints (registry).** Randomised, double-masked, placebo-controlled. Separate sporadic and C9orf72-ALS cohorts, plus an open-label extended-dosing period. Primary endpoint: participants with treatment-emergent and serious adverse events through day 421. Secondary endpoints: plasma pharmacokinetics. Efficacy is exploratory.

**Available results.** All results are *company-reported* interim data. QurAlis reports target engagement (higher STMN2 and corrected mis-splicing in tissue), a statistically significant neurofilament reduction in the low-dose part of the dose-range-finding phase, and a trend toward slower ALSFRS-R decline. A significant ALSFRS-R effect appears only in a *post hoc* sporadic subgroup that excluded the highest baseline NfL tertile (*trade press*: [NeurologyLive, Nov 2026](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/quralis-reports-anqur-interim-data-stathmin-2-targeting-agent-qrl-201-als)). The coverage gives no effect sizes, confidence intervals or adverse-event counts. We found no peer-reviewed publication and no results posted on the registry. The company plans a pivotal trial in 2027.

**Key uncertainty.** The clinical signal rests on a post hoc subgroup in a small phase 1. The neurofilament effect is reported for the low dose rather than across doses. It is also unclear whether restoring one TDP-43-dependent transcript is enough, given that TDP-43 loss mis-processes many others.

**Next question worth investigating.** How was target engagement measured? "Tissue analysis" suggests post-mortem samples, so how many participants and which doses were involved? And what do the prespecified intention-to-treat analyses of the full dose-range-finding phase show, including dose–response, for NfL and ALSFRS-R?

## 2. AP-101 (AL-S Pharma): antibody against misfolded SOD1

| | |
|---|---|
| Registry record | [NCT05039099](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05039099). Status in snapshot: completed. Phase 2. n = 73 (actual). Primary completion 2025-08-13 (*actual*). The snapshot records that results are posted on ClinicalTrials.gov; we did not review them in this pass. |
| Navigator labels | SOD1 / Antibody. Curated direct target **SOD1**, at the protein level: the antibody binds misfolded SOD1 and does not lower SOD1 expression. |

**Biological rationale.** AP-101 is a fully human IgG1 antibody that binds misfolded SOD1 selectively and spares the natively folded protein (*conference abstract*: [AAN](https://www.aan.com/MSA/Public/Events/AbstractDetails/48199)). The protocol argues that misfolded SOD1 occurs in both SOD1-mutant and sporadic ALS, citing transgenic-mouse efficacy data ([protocol, ClinicalTrials.gov](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/99/NCT05039099/Prot_000.pdf)). How far misfolded-SOD1 biology extends into sporadic ALS is the central scientific question for this programme. We did not review the literature on that question in this pass.

**Trial design and endpoints (registry and trade press).** Randomised 2:1, double-blind, with two parallel cohorts (52 sporadic, 21 SOD1-mutant). The placebo-controlled period ran 24 weeks and was followed by open-label treatment. The registered primary endpoints are safety endpoints (TEAEs and SAEs, safety findings, anti-drug antibodies). Secondary endpoints include PK, CSF AP-101, CSF pNfH and serum NfL.

**Available results.** All are *company-reported*. AL-S Pharma reports that the primary safety endpoint was met ([Sept 2025](https://www.swissbiotech.org/news/al-s-pharma-announces-positive-topline-results-from-phase-2-study-of-ap-101-for-the-treatment-of-amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-als/)). Topline data presented at AD/PD (March 2026), summarised in trade press ([Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/?p=250630)):
- a significant reduction in CSF pNfH at 6 months in the sporadic cohort (per-protocol population);
- no separation on ALSFRS-R in the overall population;
- a reported survival/NIV benefit at 12 months in both cohorts.

Body-weight differences reported later are *post hoc* (*trade press*: [NeurologyLive, Nov 2026](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/al-s-pharma-details-planned-phase-3-trial-design-ap-101-als)). A phase 3 (AP101-03) is planned for 2027. Its reported enrolment target differs between sources (about 300 vs about 450).

**Key uncertainty.** Placebo patients could cross over to AP-101 after 6 months, so any 12-month comparison is no longer a clean randomised contrast. Several highlighted effects are per-protocol or post hoc, and the SOD1-mutant cohort is small (n = 21).

**Next question worth investigating.** Read the results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov, which the snapshot flags as available. Do the intention-to-treat secondary outcomes (pNfH, NfL) match the company's per-protocol statements? And how was the 12-month survival/NIV analysis defined, given the crossover?

## 3. CNM-Au8 (Clene): oral suspension of gold nanocrystals

| | |
|---|---|
| Registry records | [NCT04098406](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04098406) (RESCUE-ALS; completed; results posted). [NCT04414345](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04414345) (HEALEY Regimen C; completed; results posted). [NCT05299658](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05299658) (open-label extension; active, not recruiting; primary completion 2026-06, *estimated*, month precision). CNM-Au8 is also listed in the HEALEY master protocol, [NCT04297683](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04297683). |
| Navigator labels | Mitochondria & cell stress / **Inorganic nanocrystal**. v1 labelled the modality Other biologic. No single gene target. |

**Composition and rationale.** The registry describes CNM-Au8 as an aqueous suspension of clean-surfaced, faceted nanocrystals of elemental gold in buffered water ([NCT05299658](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05299658)). It is not a protein or other biologic, which is why the modality was changed. The proposed mechanism is catalytic: oxidation of NADH, raising the NAD+/NADH ratio and supporting cellular bioenergetics. This mechanism comes from sponsor-affiliated preclinical work ([Wang et al., preprint later published in *Small*](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.05.08.539856.full.pdf)). The mechanism label therefore records the sponsor's proposal; it has not been independently established.

**Trial designs and endpoints (registry).**
- RESCUE-ALS: randomised, quadruple-masked, 30 mg vs placebo for 36 weeks. Primary endpoint: an EMG measure (motor unit number index).
- HEALEY Regimen C: 161 participants; co-primary ALSFRS-R and mortality over 24 weeks, analysed in a Bayesian shared model.

**Available results.**
- RESCUE-ALS missed its primary endpoint: LS-mean difference 7.7% (95% CI −11.9 to 27.3; P = .43).
- HEALEY Regimen C showed no benefit on its primary outcome: disease rate ratio 0.97 (95% CI 0.783–1.175), posterior probability of benefit 0.65. This result is *peer-reviewed*, JAMA 2025;333(13):1138–49, as cited by [NeurologyLive](https://www.neurologylive.com/view/clene-submits-nda-seeking-accelerated-approval-als-drug).
- Survival and NfL "benefits" come from open-label extensions and post hoc or responder analyses, including comparisons with external real-world controls. These are *company-reported*. Clene itself describes the responder analyses as post hoc and exploratory, and says neither randomised trial met its primary endpoint ([8-K summary](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/CLNN/8-k-clene-inc-reports-material-event-4fe336a610bc.html)).
- Clene announced an NDA submission for accelerated approval, based on NfL, on 19 October 2026. A confirmatory phase 3 is planned for Q1 2027 (*trade press*, same NeurologyLive article). We have no information on the FDA's review.

**Key uncertainty.** Two randomised trials missed their primary endpoints. "NfL responder" analyses can be confounded: patients whose NfL falls may be slower progressors whatever their treatment. Open-label survival comparisons also lose randomisation.

**Next question worth investigating.** Does either trial show a prespecified, *randomised* (placebo-controlled period) effect of CNM-Au8 on plasma NfL? Check the posted ClinicalTrials.gov results and the JAMA HEALEY paper. That is the evidence an NfL-based accelerated-approval argument would depend on.
